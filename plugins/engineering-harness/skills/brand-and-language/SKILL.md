# Brand and Language

Own product identity and differentiation at the proposal/accepted-contract level,
including visual intent, conceptual vocabulary, naming, voice and trust. Interface
Design owns the concrete composition and craft that expresses that intent.

1. Establish audience, product promise, surface purpose and accepted identity from
   repository documents and actual examples. Distinguish evidence-backed identity
   from an appealing invented story. Reuse accepted decisions rather than asking
   for approval again or generating a new brand book.
2. For identity/visual-intent work, diagnose what feels incoherent and why it matters
   to this product. Propose the smallest change to accepted intent: atmosphere,
   distinctive qualities, visual roles, vocabulary, or tone by context. Explain
   what carries across surfaces and what appropriately differs. Operational health
   tasks, reflective review and persuasive marketing need not share one intensity.
3. A new or materially changed identity, visual direction, promise, persona, naming
   system or cross-surface contract requires the existing scope-expansion approval.
   Present the concrete proposal and limits before changing production or declaring
   it authority. Approval to improve a screen or make copy friendlier is not that
   approval. For documenting/merging accepted identity, use
   [design authority](../interface-design/references/design-authority.md).
4. For wording, inspect the full action/state path and read
   [language and trust](references/language-and-trust.md) when terminology, tone,
   consequences or recovery remain unclear. Preserve facts, permissions and product
   semantics. Say what the user can do and expect without exposing mechanics that
   do not help their decision.
5. Apply authorized language or accepted identity within its limits. Request UI
   reasoning only for an unresolved implementation-level visual decision. Meaningful
   visual claims follow [rendered evidence](../interface-design/references/rendered-evidence.md);
   ordinary wording corrections do not require an evidence campaign.

Done means coherent accepted intent and language, or a bounded proposal awaiting
approval—not invented slogans, mascots, campaigns or claims. Include concrete
examples where they clarify the decision, without manufacturing user research or
clinical reassurance. Consult relevant [engineering invariants](../../references/invariants.json)
only for unresolved obligations and [precedence](../../references/precedence-and-exceptions.md)
for conflicts. Read-only reviews preserve source; required independent review uses
the existing fresh-context contract.
